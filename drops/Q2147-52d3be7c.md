# 1. Strict fundamental sector and the `p=5` obstruction

## 1.1 The Section 5 boundary lemma is correct

`paper/paper3.tex`, lines 520–540, proves:

```text
N(alpha) = -p, p prime, p != 5
    => R(alpha) != rho^j for every j in Z.
```

The argument is exact.  If `R(alpha)=rho^j`, put `gamma=eps^{-j} alpha`.  Then `R(gamma)=1`; since the norm is negative, the two real embeddings have opposite signs, so equality of their absolute values gives `Tr(gamma)=0`.  For `gamma=a+b phi`, this says `2a+b=0`, hence

```text
gamma = -a sqrt(5),
N(gamma) = -5 a^2.
```

Thus `-p=-5a^2`; for rational prime `p`, the only boundary prime is `p=5` (and then `|a|=1`).  In particular a prime with

```text
p % 10 = 1
```

cannot be `5`, so **every norm-`-p` element occurring in the finite prime theorem is strictly off both fundamental-sector boundaries**.

There is no hidden second boundary.  The two paper boundaries `R=1` and `R=rho` are unit translates of the same boundary orbit, and the statement `R(alpha) != rho^j` excludes all of them at once.

## 1.3 Existence and uniqueness in Lean are real theorems

The same file proves `exists_sameWindowSign_zpow` by a strong-induction descent on the integral measure

```lean
|Tr x| + |windowComp x|,
```

and defines

```lean

  lift : Int
```

Then, at approximately lines 254–267, it proves the exact requested uniqueness theorem:

```lean
    (c d : GeometricSectorCert x) : c.lift = d.lift := by
```

This is not proof irrelevance and not a vacuous certificate trick.  It uses the substantive theorem that every nonzero unit shift of a strict same-sign fundamental-window point has opposite window signs, composes the two proposed lifts, and contradicts the second certificate's strict normalization.

Immediately afterwards `exists_geometricSectorCert` constructs such a certificate for **every** norm-`-p` generator at `p % 10 = 1`.

So on the user's point (2): **`GeometricSectorCert.lift_unique` is genuinely proved, and the strict existence/uniqueness formalization is sound.**

# 2. One correction in the density boundary argument

There is, however, a scope error in how the paper reuses the Section 5 lemma in the density section.

## Defect 1 — `paper/paper3.tex:957–959`

Current text:

```tex
unique one. By Lemma~\ref{lem:no-boundary}, a prime element on a
boundary ray generates the prime above $5$; away from that prime we
may use the open inequalities $1<R<\rho$.
```

The conclusion is true, but **it does not follow from the cited lemma as stated**.  Lemma `no-boundary` assumes

```text
N(alpha) = -p for a rational prime p.
```

At lines 942ff of the density section, `pi_mathfrak p` has been defined for *every* nonzero prime ideal away from `2,5`.  Before the later degree-one restriction, an inert rational prime gives a degree-two prime ideal, and a prime element generating it has absolute norm `q^2`, not rational-prime norm `q`.  Thus the citation is formally too narrow at exactly the point where it is used.

The needed extension is easy and does not alter the result: if **any prime element** lies on a boundary ray, unit-normalize it to `gamma` with `R(gamma)=1`.  The same trace computation gives `gamma=m sqrt(5)`.  Since multiplication by a unit preserves primality, `gamma` is still a prime element.  If `|m|>1`, then `m sqrt(5)` factors nontrivially; hence `|m|=1`, so its principal ideal is the ramified prime above `5`.

### Exact replacement

Replace `paper/paper3.tex:957–959` by:

```tex
unique one.  If a prime element lies on a boundary ray, unit-normalizing
it as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:no-boundary} gives
$\gamma=m\sqrt5$ with $m\in\ZZ$.  Since $\gamma$ is still a prime
element, primality forces $|m|=1$, so its principal ideal is the unique
prime above $5$.  Thus away from that prime we may use the open
inequalities $1<R<\rho$.
```

With this replacement, the boundary step is valid for the full prime-ideal count, not only its degree-one subfamily.

This does **not** affect the earlier Section 5 result: for the main finite theorem, `p % 10 = 1` already excludes the unique boundary rational prime `5`.

# 5. Density deduction: cutoff and constants

Subject to the external Hecke--Mitsui/Kai input, the density arithmetic is correct.

## 5.1 Fixed lower radial cutoff: PASS

Kai's Corollary 4.2.3 (arXiv:2209.11816v2) assumes `1 < N' < N` and

```text
Norm(q) < exp(sqrt(log N') / O_K(1)).
```

Here the modulus

```text
m = (2 sqrt(5))
```

is fixed.  Therefore one may choose a single sufficiently large **fixed** `N'` once and for all and then let `N` (the paper's `X`) tend to infinity.  Removing the finitely many prime elements with norm below that cutoff changes an unweighted counting function by `O(1)`.  This part of `paper3.tex` is valid.

The finite subdivision into sufficiently small open projective charts is also legitimate in this two-real-embedding case: the modulus is fixed, only finitely many charts are needed, and internal cut rays may be chosen to avoid every nonzero lattice point.  After Defect 1 is repaired, the two outer rays cause only the prime above `5`.

Relevant external source: <https://arxiv.org/html/2209.11816>, Corollary 4.2.3.

## 5.2 Twelve classes: PASS

For

```text
m = (2 sqrt(5)),
```

CRT gives

```text
(O_K/m)^× ≅ (O_K/2)^× × (O_K/(sqrt5))^×
          ≅ F_4^× × F_5^×,
```

hence

```text
3 * 4 = 12
```

reduced classes.  With one canonical prime element per prime ideal, the twelve residue classes partition the prime ideals away from `m`.  Kai/Mitsui gives the same leading constant for each reduced class; summing and comparing with the prime ideal theorem gives

```text
12 C = 1,
C = 1/12.
```

The possible fixed-modulus exceptional-zero term is `O(X^beta)` with a fixed `beta<1`, hence is `o(X)` and does not alter the leading constant.

## 5.3 Six cells for `p ≡ 1 mod 10`: PASS

For a degree-one prime ideal of norm `p`, the canonical generator has norm `-p`.  Modulo `sqrt5`, if its class is `s`, then

```text
s^2 ≡ -p (mod 5).
```

The condition `p ≡ 1 (mod 5)` is therefore exactly

```text
s^2 ≡ -1 (mod 5),
```

whose solutions in `F_5^×` are `s=2,3`.  The mod-2 component is automatically nonzero for a prime away from `2`, so the rational norm is odd.  Thus the relevant degree-one prime ideals are exactly

```text
3 choices of ell * 2 choices of s = 6 cells.
```

Conversely, a degree-one prime ideal in one of these cells lies over an odd rational prime congruent to `1 mod 5`, hence `1 mod 10`.

## 5.4 `I_i = 1/6 Li`: PASS

For the canonical sector, `delta=0`, so `iota=-lambda=-ell`.  At each fixed `ell` there are exactly two allowed `s`-cells.  Therefore

```text
I_i(X) = 2 * (1/12) Li(X) + o(X/log X)
       = (1/6) Li(X) + o(X/log X).
```

Summing over all three `i` gives `1/2 Li(X)` degree-one prime **ideals**, as it should: rational primes `p ≡ 1 mod 10` have density `1/4` and each supplies two split prime ideals.

## 5.5 Transfer to rational primes: PASS

Reflection sends

```text
0 <-> 2,
1 -> 1.
```

Magnitude two is exactly the `(1,1)` pair.  Hence each magnitude-two rational prime contributes **two** class-1 prime ideals:

```text
I_1(X) = 2 P_2(X),
P_2(X) = (1/12) Li(X) + o(X/log X).
```

A magnitude-one rational prime has classes `(0,2)` or `(2,0)`, i.e. exactly one class-0 and one class-2 ideal:

```text
I_0(X) = I_2(X) = P_1(X),
P_1(X) = (1/6) Li(X) + o(X/log X).
```

Thus

```text
P_1 + P_2 = 1/6 + 1/12 = 1/4,
```

matching the Dirichlet progression constant for `p ≡ 1 mod 10`, and the relative densities are

```text
(1/12)/(1/4) = 1/3,
(1/6)/(1/4)  = 2/3.
```

I find **no missing factor of 2, 6, or 12** in this chain.

The deletion of degree-two prime ideals is also harmless: their norms are squares of rational primes, so norm `≤X` implies underlying rational prime `≤sqrt(X)`, giving `O(sqrt(X)/log X)`, negligible against `X/log X`.
